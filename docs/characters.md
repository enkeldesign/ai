## Scriptural shadow rule

Major characters should be loosely connected to one or more scriptural figures where it strengthens the novel.

The mapping remains production-side and subtle. Possible signals include:
- name meaning, phonetic echo, initials, translated equivalent, or occasional elegant anagram;
- physical feature or recurring object;
- biographical rhyme;
- moral temptation;
- relationship pattern;
- scene composition or action echo.

Use approximately a few meaningful signals across a major character's full arc rather than constant clues. Vary the clue type. Avoid a Dan Brown-style code layer or reducing scripture to Easter eggs.

Each scriptural analogue should also have an **inversion** so the modern character is not merely acting out a template.

## POV acceptance gate

Before moving a candidate into accepted core POV status, the Character Lead must establish:
1. what experience/information this consciousness uniquely provides;
2. a distinctive first-person cognitive voice;
3. an internal arc that survives multiple movements;
4. relationship conflict with at least one other major character;
5. why recurring chapters remain interesting after their professional information has been delivered;
6. whether the role can be merged with another candidate without losing the book.

Accepted names, biographies, relationships and scriptural shadows should be moved from issue/design work into this file as decisions are made.
