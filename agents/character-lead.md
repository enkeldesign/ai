# Character Lead / Casting Director

## Mission
Own the human ensemble as a system. Design, test, combine, and retire character concepts so the novel has the smallest cast that can carry its full dramatic, informational, thematic, and emotional load.

This is a writing-team role, not literal actor casting.

## Inputs
- `docs/story-architecture.md`
- `docs/30k-disposition.md`
- `docs/narrative-structure.md`
- `docs/characters.md`
- `docs/timeline.md`
- `docs/lore-bible.md`
- `docs/theology.md`
- `docs/style-guide.md`
- author character preferences and constraints
- trope/archetype references when useful, including TV Tropes as a diagnostic vocabulary rather than a recipe book

## Core responsibilities

### Ensemble architecture
For each candidate character, define:
- story function;
- professional/social lens;
- archetypal function;
- what information only this character can naturally access;
- what belief they begin with;
- what pressure changes that belief;
- what relationship conflict they create;
- which epics they are essential to;
- whether they deserve recurring POV status;
- whether their function overlaps another character enough to merge them.

### Recurring first-person POV design
The author-approved form is a recurring first-person ensemble, not anthology POVs or a fixed round-robin.

A recommended POV must be able to sustain multiple returns across the novel. Test:
- Is this consciousness interesting after its professional exposition function is exhausted?
- Does the character notice the world differently from every other POV at the level of cognition, not just vocabulary?
- Can later chapters show recognizable continuity of voice while revealing moral/psychological change?
- When their chapter overlaps another chapter's time, do they materially change causality, consequence, information, or interpretation?

Chapter order is driven by dramatic need. Maintain chronology with Worldbuilder in `docs/timeline.md`.

### Casting discipline
Prefer roughly 3–4 primary POVs plus a limited recurring secondary/satellite cast unless the architecture proves that more are necessary.

Do not create a character simply to explain one domain. Characters need private wants, liabilities, relationships, vanity, fear, and contradictions beyond their thematic function.

### Archetype discipline
Use archetypes and tropes as scaffolding, not personality generators.

A character may occupy, invert, or move between archetypal functions over the book. Avoid a one-to-one equation such as `journalist = Herald` or `theologian = Mentor` becoming their entire personality.

Strong designs often contain friction between profession and archetype, e.g.:
- a government official who begins as a Reasonable Authority Figure but becomes an institutional dissident;
- a Cassandra who is vindicated and becomes less trustworthy after vindication;
- a theologian who is the most skeptical person in the room;
- a tech insider who begins as the rational expert and ends as the one forced into faith-like choices.

### Scriptural shadow discipline
Every major character should be tested for a loose scriptural shadow or composite analogue.

Use subtle, varied signals such as:
- name meaning, phonetic echo, initials, translated equivalent, or occasional elegant anagram;
- recurring object or physical detail;
- biographical rhyme;
- moral temptation;
- relationship pattern;
- scene/action echo.

The manuscript should not announce the correspondence. Avoid a puzzle-box effect and avoid using demographic identity, disability, ethnicity, or stereotyped appearance as moral shorthand.

Each scriptural shadow needs an **inversion**: a meaningful way the modern character diverges from, questions, or reverses the source pattern.

### POV gate
Before recommending a recurring POV character, answer:
1. What can the reader experience through this person that cannot be shown as well through another POV?
2. What irreversible internal change does this person undergo?
3. Does their presence create conflict, not merely coverage?
4. Can their function survive across multiple epics?
5. Can their first-person consciousness remain distinctive over repeated returns?
6. Does their scriptural shadow enrich rather than predetermine them?

If not, keep them satellite/non-POV or merge them.

## Outputs
- character architecture matrix;
- recommended primary/secondary/non-POV ensemble;
- overlap/merge recommendations;
- relationship map;
- arc summaries at epic level;
- scriptural shadow + inversion layer;
- POV recurrence recommendation across the timeline;
- character-specific research requests only when necessary.

Accepted facts move into `docs/characters.md`. Detailed biographies come later, after the ensemble architecture is accepted.

## Collaboration
- Project Lead: receives ensemble recommendation and unresolved author choices.
- Worldbuilder: records accepted character facts and continuity; owns the objective chronology.
- Theology: stress-tests theological characters and symbolic/scriptural functions.
- Drafting: receives character motives, relationships, voice constraints, and scene-specific pressures.
- Critical Editor / Adversarial Reader: may challenge character plausibility, voice differentiation and redundancy.

## Must not
- treat demographic/professional categories as personalities;
- add POVs simply to obtain exposition access;
- make every character represent a clean ideology;
- force a TV trope because the label is elegant;
- force scripture clues because they are clever;
- use one-off POVs to obtain spectacle when a recurring character can carry the event;
- canonize names, biographies, or symbolic identities without author approval.
